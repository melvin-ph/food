Safe Redistribution Protocols

Food Safety & Hygiene Standards

Protecting recipients, community kitchens, and hospitality donors through strict adherence to hygiene, temperature control, and transparency.

Important Safety Disclaimer

FoodShare is a technological facilitation platform connecting food providers with charitable entities and recipients. FoodShare does not inspect or independently certify food items. Donors legally certify that listed surplus items were prepared under sanitary conditions and comply with local health codes. Recipients must inspect items upon collection and adhere to proper food storage and reheating standards.

1. Donor Responsibilities & Standards

  • Freshness & Shelf Life: Only donate food that was prepared on the same day or is well within its labeled "Best-Before" or "Use-By" date.
  • Temperature Control: Hot foods must be held at or above 65°C (149°F). Chilled perishable foods must be maintained below 4°C (40°F).
  • Allergen Disclosures: Clearly declare common allergens including peanuts, tree nuts, dairy, soy, gluten, shell fish, and eggs on every listing.
  • Packaging: Surplus meals must be sealed in clean, food-grade containers, aluminum foil trays, or insulated food-safe boxes to prevent contamination during transit.
  • Listing Cancellation: If any food is suspected to have spoiled or missed safe handling windows, the donor must immediately cancel the listing on the portal.

2. Recipient & NGO Collection Guidelines

  • Prompt Collection: Arrive strictly within the donor's agreed pickup window to maintain cold/hot chain integrity.
  • Transport Hygiene: Utilize clean insulated delivery bags, thermal coolers, or food-grade storage crates during transport.
  • Visual & Sensory Check: Always inspect packaging seals, aroma, and temperature before accepting custody of surplus food.
  • Immediate Reheating or Chilling: Cooked food should be reheated thoroughly to at least 74°C (165°F) before consumption or refrigerated immediately.

3. Permitted vs. Restricted Foods

Permitted Surplus Items Prohibited Items
Fresh commercial banquet & catering surplus Plate waste or half-eaten patron leftovers
Intact fresh vegetables, fruits, and produce Dented, bloated, or unsealed canned goods
Sealed bakery goods, bread, and pastries Expired high-risk seafood or poultry
Unopened packaged dry groceries and pulses Homemade uninspected canned preparations

Notice any food listing that violates these rules?

Use the "Report Listing" button on any donation details page to initiate immediate administrator review.
